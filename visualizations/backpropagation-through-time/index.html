Backpropagation Through Time: Full and Truncated

A visual explainer by Alec Helbling

Full backpropagation through time carries gradients from the loss through every recurrent application of the shared core. This allows later losses to shape earlier hidden states, but requires retaining or recomputing activations across the rollout.

Truncated backpropagation limits the backward path to one or a few recurrent applications. Here, gradients pass through only the final application, stopping at the detached hidden state. This reduces training costs, but prevents the loss from directly teaching earlier applications what information to preserve for later computation.
