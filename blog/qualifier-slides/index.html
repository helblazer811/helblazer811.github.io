What is a Normalizing Flow?

A Normalizing Flow transforms a simple distribution p(z)p(z)p(z) into a complex distribution p(x)p(x)p(x) by a sequence of mappings fi(z)f_i(z)fi​(z).

Rezende, D. & Mohamed, S. (2015). Variational Inference with Normalizing Flows. Proceedings of the 32nd International Conference on Machine Learning, in Proceedings of Machine Learning Research 37:1530-1538.

Continuous Normalizing Flows (CNF)

CNFs replace discrete transformations with continuous

Chen et al., Neural Ordinary Differential Equations, 2019

Sampling Trajectories From a CNF

Individual samples have trajectories x(t)\color{#f17720}{x(t)}x(t) from x0∼p0x_0 \sim p_0x0​∼p0​ to x1∼p1x_1 \sim p_1x1​∼p1​.

CNFs Learn to Represent Velocity Fields

CNFs model a velocity field vθ\color{#3b82f6}{v_\theta}vθ​.

Generate sample trajectories by solving an ODE:

dxdt=vθ(x,t)\frac{d\color{#f17720}{x}}{dt} = \color{#3b82f6}{v_\theta(x, t)}dtdx​=vθ​(x,t)

Using numerical integration methods like Euler's method:

xt+Δt=xt+Δt⋅vθ(xt,t)\color{#f17720}{x_{t+\Delta t}} \color{#333}{=} \color{#f17720}{x_t} \color{#333}{+} \color{#333}{\Delta t} \color{#333}{\cdot} \color{#3b82f6}{v_\theta(x_t, t)}xt+Δt​=xt​+Δt⋅vθ​(xt​,t)

CNFs Allow More Efficient Training

Change of Variables (Normalizing Flows)

Simulating ODEs is Still Expensive

Requires solving an ODE at every training step.

Key motivation for flow matching: CNFs are expressive but training via maximum likelihood is expensive because of the trace computation and ODE simulation at every step. Flow matching avoids both.

Directly learns velocity vθ\color{#3b82f6}{v_\theta}vθ​ instead of maximizing likelihood.

The Conditional Velocity Field

The conditional velocity field vt(xt∣x1)\color{#f17720}{v_t(x_t | x_1)}vt​(xt​∣x1​) is the velocity that moves xtx_txt​ along the path toward x1x_1x1​.

Regressing the Velocity Field

Given a point xtx_txt​ we want to predict the velocity vtθ(xt)\color{#22c55e}{v_t^\theta(x_t)}vtθ​(xt​) that matches the target conditional velocity vt(xt∣x1)\color{#f17720}{v_t(x_t | x_1)}vt​(xt​∣x1​).

This objective preserves exact likelihood evaluation, enables generation, and learns a transformation of the distribution.

What is a Coupling?

A coupling is a joint distribution π(X0,X1)\pi({\color{#3b82f6}{X_0}}, {\color{#f17720}{X_1}})π(X0​,X1​) between our source π(X0)\pi({\color{#3b82f6}{X_0}})π(X0​) and target π(X1)\pi({\color{#f17720}{X_1}})π(X1​) random variables.

Coupling: pairs drawn one at a time with labels showing (x_0, x_1).

The Naive Independent Coupling

The simplest choice of coupling is the independent coupling, where π(X0)\pi({\color{#3b82f6}{X_0}})π(X0​) and π(X1)\pi({\color{#f17720}{X_1}})π(X1​) are independent of each other.

The naive independent coupling: randomly pair source and target samples.

Our Paths Crossed at the Wrong Time

The velocity field vtθ\color{#22c55e}{v_t^\theta}vtθ​ cannot accurately resolve conflicting paths — the best it can do is average. This averaging leads to curved trajectories.

Intersecting paths create conflicts for the velocity field, which must average, producing curved trajectories.

Ambiguities Lead to Curved Trajectories

The trajectories curve, but importantly, do not intersect.
