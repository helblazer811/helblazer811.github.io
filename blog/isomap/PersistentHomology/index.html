Persistent Homology and Simplicial Complexes

Beyond Isomap and other dimensionality reduction techniques, another powerful approach to understanding the topology and structure of data comes from persistent homology\text{persistent homology}persistent homology, a method from computational topology. Rather than embedding data into low-dimensional space, persistent homology allows us to identify topological features of our data at multiple scales.

The key idea is to build a Rips complex\text{Rips complex}Rips complex, which is constructed by connecting points whose ε\varepsilonε-neighborhoods (epsilon balls) overlap. As ε\varepsilonε increases from 0, the structure of this complex changes, and topological features like connected components, loops, and voids appear and disappear. By tracking these events across all epsilon values, we can identify the "persistent" features that are most important to the underlying data.

In the visualization below, you can see how the simplicial complex evolves as epsilon increases:

  • Blue circles represent the ε\varepsilonε-balls around each data point. These grow larger as epsilon increases.
  • Orange edges connect points whose balls overlap, forming the 1-skeleton of the Rips complex.
  • Orange triangles represent 2-simplices (triangles) formed when three balls mutually overlap, filling in the 2-skeleton of the complex.

The opacity of both the balls and triangles decreases as epsilon increases, giving visual emphasis to the finer-scale features that emerge at smaller epsilon values.
