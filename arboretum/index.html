A Visual Interface for Generative Decision Tree Classifiers

Alec Helbling, Sujata Duwal, Yuhan Wang

"If you want to know why the tree is growing upside down, ask the computer scientists who introduced this convention. The conventional wisdom is that they never went out of the room, and so they never saw a real tree." — Donald Knuth

Introduction

Modern machine learning systems are increasingly deployed in safety-critical settings, such as medical diagnosis and autonomous vehicles, where decisions must be understood, justified, and revised by human stakeholders. In visual domains, this challenge is particularly salient as models must learn to both represent abstract visual features and reason about them. The rapid development of vision-language models (VLMs) has enabled numerous exciting new applications, as these models can generalize to a variety of vision tasks with minimal additional supervision. However, like existing deep learning approaches, VLMs remain difficult for non-experts to interpret, hindering stakeholders' ability to trust, audit, and intervene on their decision-making processes.

In contrast to the opaque representations of neural networks, decision trees are a classic machine learning technique often touted for their interpretability. Decision trees explicitly encode their decision-making process through a hierarchy of decision nodes which divide samples based on specific features, eventually culminating in a final prediction. Leveraging their salient decision-making process, we propose Generative Decision Trees (GDTs), a novel approach to adapting VLMs that combines the interpretability of decision trees with the powerful perceptual capabilities of VLMs. Through GDTs, we construct decision trees where the "features" are posed as simple natural language questions to vision-language models, thus benefiting from the powerful few-shot reasoning capabilities of these models.

In this paper, we present the design of an interactive visualization system that visualizes GDTs' decision-making process end-to-end. Our system contains two parts: a programming interface for users to specify the structure of trees through a human-readable, domain-specific programming language, and a visualization of the decision tree structure. Through our interactive interface, non-technical domain experts can easily modify decision trees, understand their structure, and visualize their decision-making process dynamically.

Related Works

While the development of multimodal large language models is advancing rapidly, understanding their internal mechanisms remains complex. It is critically important to foster transparency to address pivotal challenges related to fairness, clarity, ethical implications, truthfulness, and trustworthiness. Despite the well-established necessity of transparency in VLMs, the field remains in active development through various explainability methods. Our work draws on the well-established transparency of decision trees, leveraging their hierarchical, rule-based structure to make VLM reasoning accessible to both expert and non-expert audiences alike.

Vision-Language Models and Explainable AI

Vision-language models have become a popular technique for computer vision applications due to their ability to generalize to new tasks with minimal supervision. However, desired interpretability of deep learning models derives from both transparency and ad hoc explainability, which remains lacking in most VLMs, undermining users' trust in safety-critical settings. One way to address this gap is providing a transparent decision-making process to facilitate deployment in high-stakes applications. There have been extensive studies in explainable AI that use visualization as a medium for users to understand deep learning models' decision-making process. In this paper, we propose an interpretable decision-making system that combines VLMs with dynamic, real-time visual explanations to enable transparent and trustworthy autonomous decisions.

Visualization of Decision Trees

Decision trees are a classic ML technique that have been shown to be highly interpretable. Visualization of a decision tree starts at a root node, which then splits into branches leading to internal nodes and finally to leaf nodes. At each internal node, the model asks a specific question or applies a decision rule, and the branches correspond to the possible answers. As we move down the tree, these questions become more specific, progressively simplifying the decision until a final outcome is reached at a leaf node. The simple, clear, and flexible nature of decision trees plays a huge role in fostering intelligibility among non-technical human experts to oversee the underlying reasoning and evidence. In this paper, we harness decision trees' structured reasoning, beyond merely providing an accessible interactive visualization platform, to enable direct integration with non-technical domain expertise.
